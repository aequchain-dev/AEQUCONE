1.1 Purpose statement

╔═ PURPOSE ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
║
║  Define ONE parametric, scale-invariant, material-tier-agnostic construction framework in which an
║  axisymmetric shell sits on a standardised, mass-producible, demountable ring foundation
║  (AEQUBASE), so the same load path serves a 5 m entry shelter and a 30 m community hall, in entry
║  or premium materials, with safety designed foundation-first. Optimal-across-all elements are
║  fixed as invariants; conditional elements are switched by hazard, ground and tier.

1.2 Answers to the questions asked

┌─ Q1  IS CONICAL BEST?  PARTLY - AND THE PURE STEEP CONE IS NOT ───────────────────────────────────
│
│  • TRUE: any body of revolution (cone, dome, drum) loads identically from every bearing; no
│    "front". Same D and H, a cone takes 50% of the base shear and 33% of the overturning moment of
│    a drum (checked analytically: 1/2 and 1/3 for uniform pressure). A cone is a developable
│    surface: flat-sheet or flat-facet manufacture, no double-curved moulds.
│  • FALSE as pictured: at EQUAL USABLE FLOOR (40 m2, 2.1 m headroom) a pure 60 deg cone costs +94%
│    moment, +167% hold-down and +39% shell area compared with a 45 deg conical roof on a 2.1 m knee
│    wall (M 436 vs 225 kN.m; hold-down 261 vs 98 kN). Height costs moment; the perimeter of a pure
│    cone is unusable.
│  • The dome is a real rival (lower moment: 108 kN.m). Cone-on-knee beats it in 10 of 12 aero-
│    assumption cases (uniform weights) and 7 of 12 under safety weights, so the shell choice is
│    decided by wind-tunnel gate V1. The FOUNDATION is identical either way: that is the invariant.
│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌─ Q2  HOW SHOULD FOUNDATIONS BE LAID? ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
│
│  Not as a cast raft. Lay a RING of identical steel segments (chord 2.000 m) on screw anchors
│  driven to a logged torque, one anchor per node, with a continuous skirt fin. The ring is the
│  tension tie for the shell, the hold-down distributor and the sliding key in one part. Sequence:
│  site gate > set out > drive anchors (torque-logged) > set segments > close and torque ring >
│  skirt + compacted backfill > erect shell. About 8 working hours for the 7.7 m reference unit,
│  loadable immediately (no cure time).
│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌─ Q3  HOW ENHANCED?  (measured deltas, section 9.3) ───────────────────────────────────────────────
│
│  • Ring + anchors vs cast raft: foundation mass 34.3 t -> 1.88 t (5.5% of raft); embodied CO2e 5.8
│    t -> 1.1 t (recycled steel) or 3.6 t (virgin steel) - indicative factors [A].
│  • Constant-chord n-gon segment: 6 tooling sets -> 1; one segment section covers n = 8..48 and
│    WC1..WC4.
│  • Torque-as-proof: every anchor is load-qualified by its installation torque (Qult = Kt x T), so
│    QA is built into mass production.
│  • Capacity design: anchors carry >= 3x the computed demand on the reference unit; the foundation
│    outlasts the shell by design.
│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌─ Q4  CAN FOUNDATIONS OF CONICAL STRUCTURES BE MASS PRODUCED?  YES ────────────────────────────────
│
│  • One segment (RHS 150x100x6 x 2.0 m, 57 kg) and one anchor ladder (A1 60 | A2 100 | A3 150 | A4
│    250 kN) cover every size in the ladder. Ring diameter is set only by the NUMBER of segments: D
│    = 2.0 / sin(180/n).
│  • Precast radial-rib wind-turbine foundations exist but sit in active patent families (section
│    9.8); AEQUBASE is deliberately a different architecture (ring + screw anchors + skirt, any
│    material) pending a freedom-to-operate review.
│  • Spun-cast concrete suits tubes and piles, not 45 deg shells: rejected for the shell, kept as an
│    option for a central core tube.
│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
┌─ Q5  HEIGHT BELOW / ABOVE GROUND ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
│
│  • Above: ring top +0.15 m over grade (splash, drainage). Apex 5.98 m (H/D = 0.77). Taller costs
│    moment roughly with H squared; do not exceed the 45 deg family.
│  • Below: skirt 0.42-1.04 m by wind class; anchors >= 3.0 m (>= 12 helix diameters). Habitable
│    sub-grade core cell only if the design water table is >= 1.5 m below floor (weight/buoyancy
│    1.33 at 1.5 m; 0.91 at 1.0 m = floats).
│  • Berm to knee height (2.1 m): base shear 48% and moment 75% of unbermed; adds drainage and
│    moisture duties.
│
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

S/Au = shell area per usable floor area (material, cost, carbon proxy). Hold-down = uplift + M/lever
- 0.9 x shell weight (kN the foundation must supply). Impact = mean normal-impact energy fraction of
a horizontal missile (1.0 = face-on); curvature and slope help in reality but ICC 500 tests are
normal-incidence, so no code credit is claimed.

Architecture           │ Wins, uniform weights │ Wins, safety-weighted
───────────────────────┼───────────────────────┼──────────────────────
Box+flat roof          │ 0.0%                  │ 0.0%
Drum+flat roof         │ 1.9%                  │ 0.3%
Hemisphere dome        │ 23.0%                 │ 16.6%
Cone 60deg             │ 0.9%                  │ 0.1%
Cone 60deg + 1.5m knee │ 0.0%                  │ 0.0%
Cone 45deg + 2.1m knee │ 74.2%                 │ 83.0%

Weights were sampled (20,000 Dirichlet draws), not chosen. Pareto: "Cone 60deg + 1.5m knee" is
dominated by the 45 deg / 2.1 m knee. ASSUMPTION FLIP TEST: varying cone lift CL 0.25-0.50, cone Cf
0.6-0.8 and dome CL 0.3-0.7 (12 cases), the cone-on-knee beats the dome in 10 (uniform) and 7
(safety-weighted) of 12. The dome wins only when the cone is aerodynamically pessimistic. Scoring of
fabrication (developable = 1.0, dome = 0.5) and membrane efficiency are judgments, not measurements.

7. LIFECYCLE DOCUMENTATION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────


7.1 User manual outline

• What the unit is and is not: design wind class label (never "proof"); occupancy rules; evacuation
  guidance
• Record pack: torque log, as-built levels, class label plate, inspection ports, spare-boss
  locations
• Do-not list: no excavation within 1.5 m of the ring; no drilling or cutting of hip ribs; no
  overhangs or lean-tos on the shell
• After any event: gust >= 80% of class, flood, earthquake, excavation nearby -> inspect before
  reuse

7.2 Maintenance schedule

Interval     │ Task                                                          │ Who
─────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────
Annual       │ visual: ring, nodes, bolts, skirt exposure, drainage, berm    │ owner
5 years      │ torque re-check 10% of bolts; level survey; sleeve inspection │ trained technician
10 years     │ excavate one node to inspect coating and anchor head; recoat  │ technician
             │ if needed                                                     │
Event driven │ full node and level inspection; replace deformed parts        │ technician + engineer

7.3 Repair and upgrade paths

• Segment or node block: unbolt, replace 1:1 (tier-agnostic interface).
• Anchor: unscrew and replace, or add a second anchor through the spare boss on N100+ blocks.
• Wind-class upgrade WC2 -> WC4: bolt on skirt extension strips; swap anchors up one class.
• Tier upgrade: swap shell facets (T1 -> T2), then ring segments 1:1 (T2 -> T3); foundation never
  re-dug.
• Coating repair: wire-brush, cold zinc, record in the log.

7.4 End-of-life disassembly

1. Strip shell facets by tier. 2. Remove skirt fins and backfill. 3. Unbolt ring segments. 4.
   Reverse-torque each anchor, log depth.
5. Sort steel; re-use or recycle. 6. Reinstate ground; no concrete remains.

9.7 Known limitations and enhancement vectors

• Sectional drag model: no vortex shedding, no tornado translation/vertical flow, no dynamic
  response. Shell buckling and ovalling not analysed.
• Soil generic; cost, labour and times are estimates [A]; EFE "renewable" only partly met by a metal
  foundation.
• Shape ranking depends on assumed lift coefficients: dome wins if the cone is aerodynamically
  pessimistic.
• T2/T3 are performance targets, unverified.
• V-1 APC-vented lantern: in the model GCpi 0.18 removes net hold-down; needs ICC 500 vent area and
  debris-rated vents.
• V-2 lighter skirt: battered anchors or partial-height skirt. V-3 composite ring (T2) for mass. V-4
  hub-and-spoke only for weak soils.
• V-5 fewer, larger nodes at n >= 36. V-6 optional berm module with drainage design.

9.8 Prior art and patent register (not legal advice; gate V5)

• Precast radial-rib / pedestal-section foundations for wind turbines are in granted families,
  examples US10876269, US10934679, US10968592, US11795653, US10724204, EP3845712A1 (status and
